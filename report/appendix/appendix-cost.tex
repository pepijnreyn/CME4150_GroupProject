\chapter{Replacement cost regression}
\label{app:cost}

Multiple linear regression relates replacement cost to wall length, heritage and disruption scores, retaining all three predictors across six quay wall replacement projects. The purpose is to supplement structural risk assessment with cost estimates that help schedule yearly works. Among critical walls with comparable urgency, estimates support comparison of replacement packages and allocation of limited municipal funding. Heritage and disruption scores are treated as equally spaced numerical ratings.

The six projects (Table~\ref{tab:dutch-quay-walls}) share a Dutch procurement,
regulatory and construction context, and each replaces an identifiable
quay-wall section. Lengths, works and costs come from public sources; the
disruption and heritage scores, from 1 (low) to 5 (high), were judged by the
team from documented site characteristics.

Length has the strongest adjusted association with cost: approximately €13,806 per additional metre, holding the other factors constant. Standardised coefficients are 0.817 for length, −0.477 for heritage and 0.305 for disruption (Figure~\ref{fig:cost-regression}). The model explains 63.7\% of cost variation (adjusted R² = 0.094). Length is not significant at 5\% (p = 0.218); neither are heritage (p = 0.429) nor disruption (p = 0.606). With six projects and three predictors, only two degrees of freedom remain for the residuals, so the results identify exploratory associations rather than established causal effects.

Forward and backward AIC selection favoured the simpler length-only model (R² = 0.457), producing an estimated replacement cost of €2.711 million for KM060065 (54.52 m), or about €50k per metre. Its 95\% confidence interval for the mean cost was −€1.801 million to €7.223 million, while the 95\% prediction interval for an individual replacement was −€5.374 million to €10.796 million. The negative bounds are financially unrealistic and demonstrate the model's limitations.

Consequently, the results provide exploratory cost associations rather than reliable standalone project budgets. Section~\ref{sec:tradeoff} therefore keeps the cost of about €35k per metre from the programme plan \parencite{utrecht_programmaplan}, which lies within the interval of the length-only model. With more projects, the same regression could estimate package costs, so that the works selected each year, plus a justified contingency, fit the available budget.
