\chapter{Probabilistic Modelling}
\label{chapter:probabilistic-modelling}

\section{Probabilistic Models}\label{sec:prob}

Chapter~\ref{chapter:risk-analysis} needs, for each of the 17 walmuren on
their original timber piles (Section~\ref{sec:data}), the probability that it
fails under the loads of the 15-year reference period of NEN~8700
\parencite{nen8700}, given that it stands today. As in \textcite{hemel2024},
time enters only through the soft shell (2026 and 2041), so spreading this
probability over the years (Sections~\ref{sec:fault-tree} and
\ref{sec:regression}) is an assumption. The team modelled it with the lateral failure mechanism that
\textcite{hemel2023,hemel2024} studied for Amsterdam quay walls, adapted to the
two cross-section types of the Utrecht walmuren \parencite{ibu2010,erfgoed156}
(Figure~\ref{fig:app-sections}).

\subsection{Limit states and random variables}\label{sec:prob-ls}

A wall fails when its body, formed by the wall, its floor and the soil on the
floor, overturns about the front pile row, or when a timber pile breaks in
bending. Overturning is described by the limit state function
\begin{equation}
g_1 = \theta_R M_W + M_\delta - S, \label{eq:g1}
\end{equation}
where $M_W$ [kNm/m] is the moment of the weight of the body about the front
pile row, $\theta_R$ [--] a model factor, $M_\delta$ [kNm/m] the moment of the
wall friction on the back of the body and $S$ [kNm/m] the moment of the earth
pressure, the net water pressure and the surcharge over the height $H$ [m]
from the werf to the underside of the floor. Pile failure is described by
\begin{equation}
g_2 = \mathrm{MOR} - \left(\theta_M \frac{32\,M}{\pi d^3} + \frac{4N}{\pi d^2}\right),
\qquad d = D_0 - 2\bigl(s_0 + r\,(t - 2026)\bigr), \label{eq:g2}
\end{equation}
where MOR [kPa] is the bending strength of the timber (mean 22{,}000\,kPa), $M$ [kNm] and $N$ [kN]
the moment and the axial force in the most loaded pile, $\theta_M$ [--] a
model factor, $d$ [m] the diameter of the sound core, $D_0$ [m] the original
diameter, $s_0$ [m] the soft shell in 2026, $r$ [m/yr] its growth rate and $t$
[yr] the year. The moment follows from the horizontal force that the raked
front row of type~2 does not take, in a pile on elastic springs with subgrade
modulus $k$ [kN/m$^3$] and modulus of elasticity $E$ [kPa] \parencite{hemel2023}.
Growth of the soft shell, the only deterioration process in the model, stands
in for drivers of decay such as low groundwater in the drier summers of the
KNMI'23 scenarios \parencite{knmi2023}.

The wall fails if either limit state is reached (series system). Every
modelled wall stands today, and this evidence $\mathcal{E}$ of proven strength
\parencite{hemel2024} rejects the draws in which the wall would already fail
under its own soil and water (Figure~\ref{fig:app-proven}), so that
\begin{equation}
P_f = P\left(g_1 < 0 \cup g_2 < 0 \mid \mathcal{E}\right) \approx
\frac{\sum_i \mathbf{1}[\mathrm{fail}_i]\,\mathbf{1}[\mathcal{E}_i]}{\sum_i \mathbf{1}[\mathcal{E}_i]},
\qquad \beta = -\Phi^{-1}(P_f), \label{eq:pf}
\end{equation}
where $i$ numbers the Monte Carlo draws, $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ [--] is 1 if the
event occurs in a draw and 0 otherwise, and $\Phi$ is the standard normal
distribution function. The model uses 400{,}000 draws per wall and type, for 2026
and for 2041 (Appendix~\ref{app:prob}).

Table~\ref{tab:prob-variables} lists the random variables: lognormal for
positive quantities with a multiplicative error, such as $K$ and the model
factors \parencite{jcss2001}; normal for unit weights and timber properties;
uniform where only bounds are known; Bernoulli for the wall type. The normal
surcharge follows \textcite{hemel2024}, although an extreme-value distribution
would suit a maximum load better. Only the groundwater and the surcharge vary
in time (aleatory, A); the others are fixed for a wall but unknown (epistemic,
E), so measuring them narrows $P_f$.


The parameters were estimated in three ways: from a literature mean and
coefficient of variation by the method of moments, from the data of
Section~\ref{sec:data} for the werf level and the prior probability of each
wall type, and by Bayesian updating with the evidence $\mathcal{E}$, which updates all
resistance variables and the wall type together. With a prior probability $p$
[--] of type~2 for the rak, the best estimate weighs the two types as
\begin{equation}
P_f = \sum_{T=1}^{2} P(T \mid \mathcal{E})\,P_f(T \mid \mathcal{E}), \qquad
P(T{=}2 \mid \mathcal{E}) = \frac{p\,P(\mathcal{E} \mid T{=}2)}{p\,P(\mathcal{E} \mid T{=}2) + (1-p)\,P(\mathcal{E} \mid T{=}1)},
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
where $P(\mathcal{E} \mid T)$ [--] is the fraction of the draws of type $T$ in which the
wall stands today; only the werf level could be fitted to local data.

\subsection{Dependence between the variables}\label{sec:prob-dependence}

The model treats dependence explicitly in three ways. The wall type is a
common cause of the geometry, the floor level, the rake of the front piles and
the fixity of the pile heads, and the soil class sets the means of $K$ and
$\gamma$, so all variables are conditional on these two
(Equation~\ref{eq:mixture}). One draw of $K$ enters the earth pressure, the
surcharge pressure and the wall friction, and one groundwater level enters the
soil weight, the water pressure and the buoyancy of the body. Both limit
states use the same draws: for type~2 they occur together 4.4 times as often as
if independent (Appendix~\ref{app:prob-sensitivity}). Other variables are independent for lack of data;
Gaussian-copula tests of $\rho(K,\gamma) = -0.5$ and
$\rho(\mathrm{MOR},E) = 0.8$ \parencite{jcss2001} change $\beta$ by at most
0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:sens-summary}), so independence is acceptable. Section~\ref{sec:fault-tree} adds the dependence between
walls.

\subsection{Model quality and validity}\label{sec:prob-validity}

Few inputs can be compared with data (Table~\ref{tab:prob-data}). The AHN
points on a werf are heavy-tailed, so the median is used; its error, 0.04\,m,
agrees with $\sigma = 0.05$\,m (Figure~\ref{fig:app-werf-fit}). The
BRO wells stand under the raised streets, so the model bounds the groundwater
around the canal level instead.
The soil class agrees with only 25 to 67\,\% of the borings within 150\,m, the
weakest input. The soft-shell range has a mean of 22.5\,mm, close to the
21\,mm measured in Amsterdam \parencite{hemel2023}, but no Utrecht data exist.

The strongest test is that all 17 walls stand. Type~2 stands in 95
to 100\,\% of the draws, but type~1 in only 33 to 98\,\%: the type-1 model is
miscalibrated. As the model is used to rank the walls, the test is whether the ranking holds (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}). Over the eleven variants of
Appendix~\ref{app:prob}, the Spearman rank correlation with the base ranking
has a median of 0.86. It is 0.96 or more for the correlations, the screen, the
type update and restricted use, but 0.59 to 0.72 for variants that change what
differs between walls: an uncertain soil class, no wall friction, groundwater
up to the werf, and type~1 recalibrated so that every wall stands in at least
90\,\% of the draws. This takes $\theta_R$ times 2.1,
raises $\beta$ of type~1 by 1.2 to 1.5 and shrinks the gap between the types
from 2.1 to 0.9. Only Rak~03 OZ is red in all eleven variants, Rak~05 NZ and
06 ZZ in ten. The base ranking agrees with the one incident on a modelled wall:
both walls of rak~6 were widened in 2021 to prevent collapse
\parencite{gu2023vgr,wijkbericht2024walmuur}. $P_f$ describes one
cross-section, whereas a long wall has more sections that can fail, so the
ranking understates long walls against short ones (3 to 101\,m). The Monte Carlo error is 0.01 in $\beta$
(Appendix~\ref{app:prob-mc}).

The best estimates of $\beta$ are \textminus0.06 to 1.52 in 2026 (Table~\ref{tab:prob-results}), and at most 0.12 lower in 2041. The
observed damage, pile-rot settlement and loss of the screen, is not modelled
\parencite{erfgoed156}. The model thus gives an order of magnitude and a ranking that is firm only for the weakest walls until the type, soil and groundwater are measured; a verdict under NEN~8700 needs calibration first
(Section~\ref{sec:approach-selection}).
