# CME4150 Managing Uncertainty & Data: Team Challenge Rubric 2026-27

Use this rubric to grade the group report. Score each criterion on a scale of 2 to 10, where 10 is Excellent, 6 is Sufficient and 2 is Insufficient; intermediate scores are allowed. The weight of each criterion is given in brackets. Criteria 1.1 to 4.2 (80 %) concern the report. Criteria 5.1 to 5.4 (20 %) concern team dynamics and are assessed per student, not from the report.

## Instructions for the reviewer

1. Read the full report, including all appendices.
2. For each criterion 1.1 to 4.2:
   - quote or cite the sections, tables and figures that provide the evidence;
   - check every element of the level descriptors (the AND/OR conditions) and state which elements are met, partly met or missing;
   - give a score from 2 to 10 with a short justification;
   - list concrete improvements that would raise the score, ordered by effect per hour of work.
3. Check for internal inconsistencies between chapters and appendices (numbers, thresholds, model versions, the municipality summary), unfinished text (placeholders, missing captions, unresolved citations) and conceptual errors in the probabilistic, fault tree and decision models.
4. Compute the weighted score for criteria 1.1 to 4.2 (sum of score × weight, divided by 80 %).
5. End with the five improvements that gain the most grade points.

Be critical. Judge the report as an examiner would, from an engineering and a methodological standpoint.

## Objective 1: Multivariate analysis of data

*Student is able to perform multivariate analysis of data to extract knowledge via data-driven approaches, modelling tools and uncertainty quantification methods.*

### 1.1 Problem synthesis & goal definition (5 %)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Excellent (10) | The selected goal is relevant AND is clearly stated AND the most relevant characteristics of the problem are identified, including the relevant variables AND processes AND relations between variables. AND variables are discussed in terms of their availability and uncertainty/variability. |
| Sufficient (6) | The goal is clearly stated AND the most relevant characteristics of the problem are identified, including the relevant variables AND processes AND relations between variables. |
| Insufficient (2) | The goal is not clearly stated OR the most relevant characteristics of the problem are not identified, including the relevant variables, processes or relations between variables. |

### 1.2 Assumptions, data collection and generation (10 %)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Excellent (10) | Excellent presentation of the collected data that is available for the identified problem, AND sound assumptions are indicated to support the problem analysis when data is not available. AND the impact of the assumptions on the final result is discussed, AND the required data is properly generated and presented. |
| Sufficient (6) | Adequate presentation of the collected data that is available for the identified problem, AND sound assumptions are indicated to support the problem analysis when data is not available, AND the required data is properly generated. |
| Insufficient (2) | Relevant data has not been collected OR adequate assumptions made. OR poor justification of the presented data and its relevance in the context of the project. OR important assumptions are not stated. OR the required data is not properly generated. |

### 1.3 Problem exploration and dimension reduction (5 %)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Excellent (10) | The variables are properly assessed in relation to their importance in the final results using an adequate Sensitivity Analysis (SA) AND consistent decisions are taken based on the results, data variability and uncertainty level, AND the entire process is properly justified. |
| Sufficient (6) | The variables are properly assessed in relation to their importance in the final results using an adequate Sensitivity Analysis EITHER without a proper justification of the used method OR the decisions taken are not fully consistent with the results, data variability and uncertainty level. |
| Insufficient (2) | The variables are not properly assessed in relation to their importance in the final results using an adequate Sensitivity Analysis. |

### 1.4 Approach selection (5 %)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Excellent (10) | Excellent explanation of the approach selected to achieve the goal including the modelling tools and uncertainty quantification methods AND is the most convenient approach for the addressed problem and available data. |
| Sufficient (6) | The approach selected to achieve the goal is clearly explained including the modelling tools and uncertainty quantification methods AND is adequate for the addressed problem and available data. |
| Insufficient (2) | The approach selected to achieve the goal is poorly explained OR is inadequate for achieving the goal. |

## Objective 2: Probabilistic models

*Student is able to construct relevant probabilistic models, accounting for the dependence between variables and derive consistent conclusions.*

### 2.1 Probabilistic models (15 %)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|

| Sufficient (6) | Adequate probabilistic models (uni- or multivariate) are built with explicit consideration of the dependence between random variables AND adequately validated. |

## Objective 3: Risk and reliability analysis

*Student is able to use risk and reliability analysis concepts to evaluate systems, their characteristics and behaviour to support decision-making under uncertainty.*

### 3.1 Fault tree construction (10 %)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Excellent (10) | Excellent identification and elaboration of causes leading to the occurrence of the undesired top event and critical reflection on the numerical evaluation. |
| Sufficient (6) | Adequate construction and numerical analysis of the fault tree with possible causes leading to the undesired top event and appropriate Boolean gates. |
| Insufficient (2) | Inadequate construction of the fault tree, inconsistencies and incorrect quantification of the tree. |

### 3.2 Trade-off analysis (10 %)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Excellent (10) | Excellent trade-off analysis with justified numerical evaluations of the uncertainties involved. |
| Sufficient (6) | Adequate trade-off analysis of costs and benefits of risk-reduction measures. |
| Insufficient (2) | Inadequate trade-off analysis with missing objectives, wrong numerical evaluations. |

## Objective 4: Actions and plans

*Student is able to propose actions (short-term) or plans (long-term) to address specific construction and management-related problems based on an adequate strategy for data and uncertainty management with a holistic vision of the course.*

### 4.1 Regression analysis (10 %)

| Sufficient (6) | Adequate regression analysis is used to make educated guesses to predict other outcomes and future trends. |
| Insufficient (2) | Incorrect regression analysis is used to make educated guesses to predict other outcomes and future trends. |

### 4.2 Results interpretation and proposed recommendations (10 %)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Excellent (10) | The obtained solution is adequately discussed in the context of the level of abstraction of the models, data uncertainty and assumptions made. AND specific actions and/or plans are proposed to address the identified problem based on a complex analysis of the result. AND the message is conveyed in a clear manner to non-experts. |
| Sufficient (6) | The obtained solution is poorly discussed in relation to the level of abstraction of the models, data uncertainty and assumptions made OR specific actions and/or plans are proposed to address the identified problem based on the direct analysis of the result. |
| Insufficient (2) | The obtained solution is not discussed in relation to the level of abstraction of the models, data uncertainty and assumptions made. OR the proposed actions and/or plans to address the identified problem are trivial, OR they are not supported by the results. |
