# Walmuren of the Wervengebied: data per wall

All 51 walmuren of the Wervengebied in the municipal asset register (`Kunstwerktype Utrecht` = Walmuur, name not empty). Each column is an input the strength models need per wall. A dash (–) means no data set we have contains it for that wall. Cells marked [e] are an assumption and cells marked [g] an estimate, not data.

Generated by `scripts/table_wervengebied_data.py`; rerun it after the data changes. How each value is determined: `wervengebied_data_table_README.md`.

[g] estimate, not data: H = werf level − canal bottom. The werf level is measured (AHN); the canal bottom is not, and is taken at the underside of the wall (-0.08 m NAP, Erfgoed Utrecht report 158). The bed may be deeper, so H is a lower bound. Counted apart from the data in the bottom row.  
[h] AHN shows ground above +1.6 m NAP beside this wall (street level, or a bridge or stair), so it may have no werf and H is not estimated. Check on a map. Not counted in the bottom row.  
[p] part of this wall is not renewed yet (`remaining` in `walmuur_foundation.csv`).  
[t] no renewal found in the programme documents, so assumed to be still on the original timber piles. Counted apart from the data in the bottom row.  
[u] a renewal was started or planned, but no document confirms that it was finished or which method was used. Not counted in the bottom row.

Groundwater is never measured at the wall: every value is a weighted average of the 6 nearest BRO wells, which lie a few hundred metres away (`nearest_well_m` in `objects_groundwater.csv`). The depth is below the ground level at the wells, not below the werf the walmuren stand on; `glg_m_nap` in `objects_groundwater.csv` gives the level in m NAP.

Soil type is read from the nearest DINO boring over the soil the walmuur holds back: from the werf at +1.10 m NAP down to the underside of the wall at -0.08 m NAP (both from Erfgoed Utrecht report 158, as `Hoogte` is empty). It is soft clay / peat or sand when at least 70% of that range is clay-like or sand-like, and mixed otherwise. Nearby borings often disagree and many descriptions are old, so this is a best guess per wall. `masonry_walls_soil.csv` gives the boring, the shares and how many borings within 150 m agree; it is made by `scripts/build_soil_type.py`.

Road a is the distance in plan from the wall to the first carriageway or parking bay towards the werf side, lowest and highest of 6 places along the wall (> 30 m: none that close). On the walls with a werf that road runs at street level, over the werfkelders, and not on the soil the walmuur holds back; the load on the werf itself is people (5 kN/m² in NEN-EN 1991-1-1, at a = 0). The axle limit is the posted maximum axle load of the nearest C20 sign; a sign more than about 30 m away may stand on another street. Every C20 sign near a walmuur says 2.0 t, and most canal streets are also closed to lorries (C7). Made by `scripts/build_walmuur_traffic.py`.

Foundation is read from the records of the werven programme (progress reports, archaeology reports, neighbourhood letters), per rak and bank; the register has no foundation data, and its `Aanlegjaar` is often not updated after a renewal. Steel sheet piles: old wall dug free and replaced on a sheet pile (until mid-2015 the only method). Steel tube piles: piles screwed through the old wall. A ? means the method is inferred, not stated for that rak. A renewed wall no longer stands on timber piles, so a timber decay model does not apply to it, and the screening counts its design life from the renewal year. Sources per wall: `walmuur_foundation.csv`; made by `scripts/build_walmuur_foundation.py`.
